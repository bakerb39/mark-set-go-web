The Libation Bearers

Aeschylus · Tragedy

Orestes’ return, recognition with Electra, revenge against Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, and the moral trap created by competing duties.

1. Before You Read

The second play of the Oresteia shifts attention to Agamemnon’s children. Orestes returns from exile under a command to avenge his murdered father.

2. Electra at the Tomb

The grave of Agamemnon becomes a meeting place of memory, grief, and political resistance.

3. Recognition

Orestes and Electra recognize one another and join in a plan for revenge. Recognition creates alliance but also moves the siblings toward another killing.

4. Apollo’s Command

Orestes believes Apollo requires him to avenge Agamemnon. Obedience to one duty means violating another.

5. Aegisthus

Aegisthus helped murder Agamemnon and now shares power with Clytemnestra.

6. Clytemnestra and Orestes

The confrontation between mother and son is the play’s deepest moral crisis. Orestes must choose between avenging his father and committing matricide.

7. The Furies Appear

After the murder, Orestes sees the Furies. Revenge has succeeded tactically but failed to end the moral cycle.

8. Why It Matters

The Libation Bearers shows why revenge cannot produce stable justice when every act creates a new grievance.
