The Clouds

Aristophanes · Comedy

Education, rhetoric, debt, generational conflict, Socrates as a comic figure, and Athenian anxiety about new intellectual methods.

1. Before You Read

Strepsiades is overwhelmed by debts caused partly by his son Pheidippides’ expensive tastes. He seeks intellectual training as a way to defeat his creditors through argument.

2. The Thinkery

The school associated with the comic Socrates is a parody of new intellectual culture, mixing philosophy, natural speculation, rhetoric, and sophistic education.

3. The Comic Socrates

Aristophanes’ Socrates is not a reliable portrait of the historical philosopher. He functions as a symbol of intellectual trends that worried conservative Athenians.

4. Better and Worse Arguments

The contest dramatizes conflict between traditional moral education and rhetorical cleverness capable of making weaker positions appear stronger.

5. Strepsiades

Strepsiades wants education not to become wiser, but to escape consequences. The play satirizes both teachers and consumers of cleverness.

6. Pheidippides

The son becomes more adept at argument than his father expects. Intellectual technique turns against the household that sought to exploit it.

7. The Ending

The destruction of the Thinkery expresses comic backlash against education perceived as corrosive, while leaving the audience with an uncomfortable conclusion.

8. Why It Matters

The Clouds remains useful for thinking about education, rhetoric, expertise, generational conflict, and fears that clever reasoning can detach intelligence from virtue.
