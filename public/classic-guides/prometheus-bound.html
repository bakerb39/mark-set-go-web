Prometheus Bound

Aeschylus · Tragedy

Rebellion, authority, suffering, foreknowledge, technology, and the conflict between divine power and moral resistance.

1. Before You Read

Prometheus is punished by Zeus for aiding humanity, especially by giving human beings fire and the arts associated with civilization.

2. Prometheus as Benefactor

Prometheus presents himself as the champion of fragile human beings. His gifts include fire, calculation, medicine, navigation, and technical skill.

3. Zeus and New Power

Zeus is portrayed as a relatively new ruler whose authority can appear harsh and insecure. The play asks whether power and justice are identical.

4. Defiance

Prometheus refuses submission even under torture. His resistance raises questions about when obedience becomes complicity and when rebellion becomes pride.

5. Knowledge

Prometheus possesses knowledge Zeus needs. The prisoner is physically powerless but intellectually dangerous.

6. Suffering

Prometheus’ pain becomes both punishment and testimony against the ruler who inflicts it.

7. Human Civilization

The play associates civilization with acquired arts rather than natural self-sufficiency. Human progress depends on knowledge that was, in mythic terms, stolen.

8. Why It Matters

Prometheus Bound remains powerful wherever people debate authority, resistance, scientific knowledge, technological power, and the moral cost of challenging rulers.
