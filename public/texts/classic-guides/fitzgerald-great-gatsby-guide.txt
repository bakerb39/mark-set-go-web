THE GREAT GATSBY
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Original work by F. Scott Fitzgerald

A guide to the American Dream, class, desire, memory, self-invention, money, narration, and Fitzgerald’s Jazz Age tragedy.

ABOUT THIS GUIDE
This independently written educational guide is designed for serious first reading, rereading, and seminar study. It follows the Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide pattern with focused sections, discussion prompts, section quizzes, Great Ideas, review, and whole-guide quiz hooks.

HOW TO USE IT
Read one section at a time. Use Discuss with reading companion after each section for interpretation and Quiz me for a short comprehension check. Save the full-guide quiz for the end.

REVIEW — TEN CLAIMS TO REMEMBER

1. State the work’s central problem in one sentence.
2. Identify its strongest argument, image, character relationship, or formal achievement.
3. Distinguish what the work depicts from what it endorses.
4. Track the work’s major causal, conceptual, historical, or character relationships.
5. Identify one historical assumption or limitation.
6. Explain one major symbol, scene, technique, or distinction in the work’s own framework.
7. Connect the work to at least two earlier Great Books.
8. State the strongest objection or criticism.
9. Identify one insight that survives even if some elements have aged poorly.
10. Explain why the work still belongs in a Great Books curriculum.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Which claim, scene, or image from this work most changes the way you understand its subject?

DEEPER READING LABS

LAB 1 — ARGUMENT OR PLOT MAP
Map the central movement and identify the decisive choices, reversals, or interpretive turns.

LAB 2 — KEY DISTINCTIONS
Identify five contrasts or tensions the work relies on and explain what becomes difficult to understand if each is blurred.

LAB 3 — HISTORICAL TEST
Choose one historical assumption, institution, or cultural context and evaluate how much the work depends on it.

LAB 4 — STRONGEST OBJECTION
Construct the strongest objection you can, then answer it using evidence from the work itself.

LAB 5 — GREAT BOOKS CONNECTION
Compare this work with at least two earlier authors on freedom, society, religion, art, identity, power, history, or human nature.
