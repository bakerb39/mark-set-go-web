FEAR AND TREMBLING
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Original work by Søren Kierkegaard

A guide to Abraham, faith, ethics, the absurd, inwardness, and the famous problem of the teleological suspension of the ethical.

AT A GLANCE

Writing under the pseudonym Johannes de Silentio, Kierkegaard retells the binding of Isaac in order to ask what faith means when it cannot be translated into universal ethical language. Abraham becomes a paradox: either he is a murderer or he stands in an absolute relation to God that ethics alone cannot explain.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Kierkegaard make Abraham difficult rather than presenting him as an easy model of religious obedience?

THE PSEUDONYMOUS VOICE

The narrator admires faith but repeatedly says he does not possess it. This distance matters. Fear and Trembling is not a straightforward doctrinal manual; it is a literary-philosophical experiment designed to make the reader feel the difficulty of faith.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does the narrator’s inability to understand Abraham shape the reader’s experience?

THE AKEDAH

Genesis 22 is retold from several imagined perspectives. Each version emphasizes what Abraham risks: his son, his ethical identity, his relation to Sarah, and his ability to explain himself. The repetitions prevent the biblical story from becoming emotionally familiar or morally easy.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Kierkegaard repeatedly retell the same biblical event from different angles?

THE TRAGIC HERO

The tragic hero sacrifices something precious for a universal ethical end. Agamemnon can explain the sacrifice of Iphigenia through public duty. Abraham cannot appeal to a universal reason because the command is radically personal.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What makes Abraham different from a tragic hero who sacrifices for the community?

THE KNIGHT OF INFINITE RESIGNATION

Infinite resignation gives up the finite object while preserving it inwardly through eternal commitment. This movement is philosophically intelligible: one renounces the impossible. But Kierkegaard says faith makes a second movement beyond resignation.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is resignation profound but still not identical with faith?

THE KNIGHT OF FAITH

The knight of faith gives up the finite and nevertheless believes, by virtue of the absurd, that the finite will be received again. Abraham both surrenders Isaac and believes he will retain Isaac. Faith therefore contains a paradox that cannot be reduced to ordinary calculation.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What is the second movement that distinguishes faith from resignation?

TELEOLOGICAL SUSPENSION OF THE ETHICAL

The book’s most famous question asks whether the ethical can be suspended for a higher telos. In Hegelian ethics, the universal has priority over the individual. Abraham appears to place the individual in an absolute relation to the absolute.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is this problem dangerous if detached from the rest of Kierkegaard’s account of fear, responsibility, and silence?

SILENCE AND DISCLOSURE

Ethics normally requires disclosure: the individual should make actions intelligible to others. Abraham cannot explain his action without either revealing something absurd or turning it into a universal rule it is not. His silence isolates him.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does the inability to communicate become part of Abraham’s trial?

ANXIETY, RESPONSIBILITY, AND IMITATION

Kierkegaard does not invite readers casually to imitate Abraham. The very extremity of the case makes religious self-deception a central danger. Someone who simply appeals to a private divine command cannot thereby prove faith.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What safeguards, if any, does the text provide against fanaticism?

FAITH AND MODERNITY

Fear and Trembling attacks the idea that Christianity has become easy simply because a society calls itself Christian. Faith cannot be inherited as social convention; it must be lived as a demanding existential relation.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Kierkegaard think a culturally Christian society may actually conceal the difficulty of faith?

LEGACY

The book became foundational for existentialism, theology, philosophy of religion, and debates about moral limits. It remains powerful because it refuses to dissolve the tension among faith, ethics, subjectivity, and responsibility.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Can faith be rationally defended without ceasing to be the kind of faith Kierkegaard describes?
