AGAMEMNON
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Aeschylus · Tragedy

GUIDE AT A GLANCE
Victory poisoned by sacrifice, inherited violence, revenge, and the collapse of a royal house.

[[MSG:SECTION]]

1. BEFORE YOU READ

Agamemnon is the first play of the Oresteia. The Trojan War is over, but the king returns to a household already shaped by murder, sacrifice, resentment, and prophecy.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

2. THE HOUSE OF ATREUS

The royal family carries a history of reciprocal violence. The tragedy assumes that crimes do not remain isolated: one act of blood generates claims for more blood.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

3. IPHIGENIA

Agamemnon’s sacrifice of his daughter Iphigenia to enable the Greek fleet to sail gives Clytemnestra a powerful motive for revenge.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

4. CLYTEMNESTRA

Clytemnestra controls the palace, language, and stage action. She is both wronged wife and calculating murderer, making moral judgment deliberately difficult.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

5. THE RED CARPET

Agamemnon’s decision to walk on luxurious tapestries is symbolically dangerous. It suggests pride and helps Clytemnestra draw him into the palace on her terms.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

6. CASSANDRA

Cassandra sees the truth but cannot persuade anyone to act on it. Her prophecy links the present murder to the older history of the house.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

7. MURDER AND AFTERMATH

Clytemnestra kills Agamemnon and Cassandra and claims justice. The play ends not with resolution but with a new demand for vengeance.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

8. WHY IT MATTERS

Agamemnon asks whether revenge can ever settle a moral account—or whether justice must eventually become something different from retaliation.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]
