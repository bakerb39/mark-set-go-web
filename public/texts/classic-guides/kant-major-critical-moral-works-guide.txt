AT A GLANCE

Kant asks two connected questions: What can we know? and What ought we to do? His critical philosophy argues that human experience is possible because the mind contributes organizing forms and concepts, yet this same structure limits speculative knowledge. His moral philosophy grounds obligation in rational autonomy rather than inclination or external authority.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How can the same philosophy both limit reason and give reason a central role in morality?

THE COPERNICAN TURN

Instead of assuming cognition must conform to objects exactly as they are in themselves, Kant asks whether objects as experienced conform to the conditions of human cognition. This makes necessary knowledge possible while limiting claims about things independently of experience.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What changes when philosophy studies the conditions under which objects can appear to us?

SPACE AND TIME

Space and time are not learned as ordinary empirical properties. Kant treats them as forms through which human sensibility receives appearances. They structure every possible outer or inner experience for us.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Kant think space and time are conditions of experience rather than discoveries made within experience?

THE CATEGORIES

Experience is not a mere stream of sensations. The understanding organizes intuitions through concepts such as causality, substance, unity, and plurality. Kant’s transcendental deduction seeks to show that these categories are valid because unified experience would be impossible without them.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is causality, for Kant, more than a habit formed by repeated observation?

PHENOMENA AND THINGS IN THEMSELVES

We know appearances as structured by human forms of intuition and understanding. Kant distinguishes phenomena from things considered independently of those conditions. The distinction limits metaphysical speculation without claiming that appearances are illusions.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How can experience be objectively valid while still being knowledge only of phenomena?

THE ANTINOMIES

When reason treats the world as a completed object beyond possible experience, it generates opposing arguments about beginnings, divisibility, freedom, and necessity. The conflicts reveal misuse of reason rather than simple lack of information.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What do the antinomies teach about questions reason cannot legitimately answer theoretically?

GOOD WILL AND DUTY

Kant begins moral philosophy with the claim that a good will is good without qualification. Moral worth does not depend simply on achieving desired consequences; it depends on acting from duty because the rational principle of the action is right.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Kant separate moral worth from success, happiness, and inclination?

THE CATEGORICAL IMPERATIVE

A hypothetical imperative tells us what to do if we want some further goal. A categorical imperative binds rational agents regardless of contingent desires. The universal-law formulation asks whether the maxim of an action could be willed as a universal law.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What is exposed when a maxim cannot be consistently universalized?

HUMANITY AS AN END

Another formulation commands us to treat humanity in oneself and others always as an end and never merely as a means. This is a ban on treating rational persons as disposable instruments whose agency does not matter.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does the humanity formula connect morality to dignity and consent?

AUTONOMY

Autonomy means rational self-legislation: the moral agent is bound by laws reason can recognize as universally valid, not by arbitrary external command. The kingdom of ends imagines rational beings as joint legislators in a moral community.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Kant think genuine moral law must be self-imposed by reason?

FREEDOM AND PRACTICAL REASON

Theoretical reason cannot prove freedom as an object of speculative knowledge, yet practical reason requires us to regard ourselves as free agents capable of obligation.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does moral responsibility change the philosophical status of freedom?

GREAT IDEAS AND LEGACY

Kant reshaped epistemology, metaphysics, ethics, political thought, aesthetics, and later philosophy. His central achievement is critical: reason must investigate its own powers and limits while recognizing the dignity of rational agency.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Is Kant’s deepest idea limitation, autonomy, universality, or the connection among all three?

REVIEW — TEN CLAIMS TO REMEMBER

1. State the central problem of the work in one sentence.
2. Identify the author’s strongest argument.
3. Distinguish description from prescription.
4. Track the major causal or conceptual relationships.
5. Identify the historical assumptions the argument depends on.
6. Explain one major term in the author’s own framework.
7. Connect the work to at least two earlier Great Books.
8. State the strongest objection to the work.
9. Identify one idea that survives even if some conclusions fail.
10. Explain why the work still belongs in a Great Books curriculum.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Which one claim from this work most changes the way you understand its subject?
