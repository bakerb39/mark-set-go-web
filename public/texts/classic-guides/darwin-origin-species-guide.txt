An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide

AT A GLANCE

Darwin explains how species can change over immense stretches of time through descent with modification. His central mechanism is natural selection: heritable variations that improve survival or reproduction tend to become more common. The book is as important for its method of cumulative argument as for its biological conclusions.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Darwin build his case from many converging lines of evidence rather than one decisive experiment?

VARIATION UNDER DOMESTICATION

Darwin begins with breeders because artificial selection makes visible a principle that also operates in nature: populations contain heritable variation, and selective reproduction can produce striking change over generations.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What does domestication show about the amount of variation hidden within a species?

VARIATION UNDER NATURE

Wild populations also vary. Darwin challenges the idea that species are perfectly fixed types and emphasizes individual differences as the raw material upon which selection acts.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is ordinary variation conceptually more important than rare monstrosities in Darwin’s theory?

THE STRUGGLE FOR EXISTENCE

Organisms produce more offspring than can survive. Limited resources, predation, climate, disease, and competition create a broad struggle for existence that includes both direct conflict and dependence on environmental conditions.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is Darwin’s 'struggle' broader than literal combat between organisms?

NATURAL SELECTION

If variations affect survival or reproduction and are inherited, favorable variants can accumulate. Selection has no foresight; it preserves differences that work under present conditions.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does natural selection produce adaptation without planning toward a future goal?

DIVERGENCE AND COMMON DESCENT

Darwin links selection to branching descent. Lineages diverge as populations adapt to different niches, producing the tree-like pattern of relationships among living things.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Darwin’s theory predict a branching tree rather than a linear ladder of progress?

DIFFICULTIES OF THE THEORY

Darwin devotes major attention to objections: complex organs, sterile worker insects, gaps in the fossil record, and apparent discontinuities among species. His willingness to foreground difficulties is part of the book’s argumentative strength.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does openly presenting hard cases strengthen rather than weaken Darwin’s scientific argument?

THE IMPERFECT GEOLOGICAL RECORD

Darwin explains why transitional forms may be rare in fossils: preservation is exceptional, strata are incomplete, and geological time is vast. Absence of evidence therefore requires interpretation rather than automatic refutation.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
When is missing evidence a genuine objection, and when can the process producing the evidence explain the gap?

GEOGRAPHICAL DISTRIBUTION

Island species, continental patterns, barriers, and migration make sense if species descend from earlier populations that dispersed and changed. Biogeography becomes evidence for history.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why are island organisms especially revealing for theories of common descent?

HOMOLOGY AND EMBRYOLOGY

Shared anatomical plans, vestigial structures, and similarities in embryonic development are difficult to explain as isolated creations but natural under descent with modification.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does common ancestry unify observations that otherwise look like disconnected curiosities?

DARWIN’S SCIENTIFIC STYLE

Darwin proceeds comparatively, historically, and probabilistically. He repeatedly asks what each theory would predict and which framework best organizes otherwise scattered facts.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What makes explanatory unification a form of evidence in historical sciences?

LEGACY AND LIMITS

Modern evolutionary biology transformed Darwin’s mechanism through genetics, population theory, molecular biology, and developmental science. Darwin lacked Mendelian genetics, but the core idea of selection acting on inherited variation survived and expanded.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Which parts of Darwin’s argument were provisional, and which became foundational?
