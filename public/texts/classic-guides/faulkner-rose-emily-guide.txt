A ROSE FOR EMILY
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Original work by William Faulkner

A guide to memory, decay, the Old South, isolation, time, community, gender, death, and Faulkner’s gothic short story.

ABOUT THIS GUIDE
This independently written educational guide is designed for serious first reading, rereading, and seminar study. It follows the Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide pattern with focused sections, discussion prompts, section quizzes, Great Ideas, review, and whole-guide quiz hooks.

HOW TO USE IT
Read one section at a time. Use Discuss with reading companion after each section for interpretation and Quiz me for a short comprehension check. Save the full-guide quiz for the end.

AT A GLANCE

The story reconstructs Emily Grierson’s life through the collective voice of her town. Its nonlinear chronology, gothic imagery, and final revelation turn a local curiosity into a meditation on memory, social pressure, isolation, and historical decay.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Faulkner tell Emily’s story out of chronological order?

REVIEW — TEN CLAIMS TO REMEMBER

1. State the work’s central problem in one sentence.
2. Identify its strongest scene, argument, character relationship, or formal achievement.
3. Distinguish what the work depicts from what it endorses.
4. Track the work’s major historical, psychological, or character relationships.
5. Identify one historical assumption or limitation.
6. Explain one major symbol, scene, technique, or distinction.
7. Connect the work to at least two earlier Great Books.
8. State the strongest objection or criticism.
9. Identify one insight that survives even if some elements have aged poorly.
10. Explain why the work still belongs in a Great Books curriculum.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
