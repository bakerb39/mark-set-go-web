AT A GLANCE

Hardy’s memoir-essay defends pure mathematics as a creative art governed by beauty, depth, and permanence. At the same time, it is intensely personal: an aging mathematician reflects on talent, decline, ambition, and the meaning of a life devoted to abstract work.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is the book both a philosophy of mathematics and an autobiography of intellectual loss?
