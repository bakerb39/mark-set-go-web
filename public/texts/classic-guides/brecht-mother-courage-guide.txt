AT A GLANCE

Mother Courage follows Anna Fierling, a canteen woman who profits from the Thirty Years’ War even as the war destroys her children. Brecht refuses sentimental identification and instead asks the audience to examine the economic structures that make war profitable and persistent.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Brecht make Mother Courage both victim of war and participant in its economy?

MOTHER COURAGE

Courage is resourceful, practical, funny, and morally compromised. Her strengths are inseparable from the system that consumes her family.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD

REVIEW — TEN CLAIMS TO REMEMBER

1. State the work’s central problem in one sentence.
2. Identify its strongest scene, argument, character relationship, or formal achievement.
3. Distinguish what the work depicts from what it endorses.
4. Track the work’s major historical, psychological, or character relationships.
5. Identify one historical assumption or limitation.
6. Explain one major symbol, scene, technique, or distinction.
7. Connect the work to at least two earlier Great Books.
8. State the strongest objection or criticism.
9. Identify one insight that survives even if some elements have aged poorly.
10. Explain why the work still belongs in a Great Books curriculum.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
