THE ODYSSEY
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Homer · Epic Poetry

GUIDE AT A GLANCE
Homecoming, identity, loyalty, temptation, intelligence, storytelling, and the restoration of order after war.

[[MSG:SECTION]]

1. BEFORE YOU READ

The Odyssey begins after the Trojan War and follows the long return of Odysseus to Ithaca. The poem is as interested in what kind of man can return home as it is in the adventures that delay him.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

2. TELEMACHUS

The opening books follow Telemachus, who must grow into responsibility while his household is consumed by the suitors.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

3. ODYSSEUS AS A HERO

Odysseus is defined less by brute strength than by intelligence, adaptability, endurance, disguise, and persuasive speech. His gifts save him repeatedly, but pride also creates danger.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

4. HOSPITALITY

The poem repeatedly tests hosts and guests. Good hospitality is a sign of civilized order; violations of hospitality reveal moral disorder.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

5. TEMPTATION AND DELAY

Calypso, Circe, the Lotus-Eaters, and the Sirens dramatize alternatives to homecoming. Odysseus must repeatedly choose memory, duty, and identity.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

6. RETURN TO ITHACA

Odysseus returns in disguise and tests servants, family, and household loyalties before revealing himself.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

7. JUSTICE AND THE SUITORS

The slaughter of the suitors is presented as restoration, but it also raises questions about vengeance, proportionality, and violence.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

8. WHY IT MATTERS

The Odyssey asks how a person survives change without losing the self, and how a damaged household and political order can be rebuilt.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

Great Ideas to carry into syntopical reading: Home · Identity · Prudence · Loyalty · Hospitality · Justice · Temptation · Order.
