THE EUMENIDES
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Aeschylus · Tragedy

GUIDE AT A GLANCE
Orestes’ trial, competing forms of justice, Athena’s intervention, and the transformation of vengeance into civic law.

[[MSG:SECTION]]

1. BEFORE YOU READ

Orestes has killed his mother Clytemnestra to avenge his father. The Furies pursue him because matricide creates a new blood claim.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

2. THE FURIES

The Furies represent an ancient form of justice rooted in kinship and blood. They are terrifying, but they enforce a genuine moral order.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

3. APOLLO AND ORESTES

Apollo defends Orestes and represents a competing divine claim. Another act of violence would only repeat the cycle.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

4. ATHENA’S COURT

Athena creates a trial in Athens. Conflicting claims are transferred from private vengeance to public judgment.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

5. THE VOTE

The divided vote dramatizes the difficulty of the case. The new system does not pretend that moral conflict has vanished.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

6. TRANSFORMATION OF THE FURIES

Athena does not merely defeat the Furies. She incorporates them into civic order and gives them honor.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

7. FROM HOUSEHOLD TO CITY

The trilogy moves from crimes inside a family toward institutions that can stabilize a political community.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]

[[MSG:SECTION]]

8. WHY IT MATTERS

The Eumenides is a great reflection on why law courts exist: societies need alternatives to endless retaliation.

[[MSG:DISCUSS]]
