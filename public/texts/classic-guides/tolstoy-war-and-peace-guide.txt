WAR AND PEACE
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Original work by Leo Tolstoy

A guide to history, freedom, family, war, love, death, leadership, and Tolstoy’s challenge to heroic theories of history.

ABOUT THIS GUIDE
This independently written educational guide is designed for serious first reading, rereading, and seminar study. It follows the Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide pattern with focused sections, discussion prompts, section quizzes, Great Ideas, review, and whole-guide quiz hooks.

AT A GLANCE

War and Peace combines family chronicle, historical novel, battlefield narrative, philosophical essay, and spiritual inquiry. Tolstoy follows several aristocratic families through the Napoleonic wars while questioning whether history is actually made by the great men to whom textbooks assign responsibility.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Tolstoy undermine heroic history while still making Napoleon and Kutuzov major characters?

PIERRE BEZUKHOV

Pierre inherits wealth without a settled identity and searches through pleasure, Freemasonry, reform, love, captivity, and spiritual awakening. His development is less a straight ascent than repeated correction.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Pierre learn more through failure and suffering than through abstract programs of self-improvement?

ANDREI BOLKONSKY

Prince Andrei seeks glory, retreats into disillusionment, rediscovers hope, and eventually confronts mortality. His changing view of Napoleon mirrors the novel’s critique of heroic ambition.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What does Andrei discover when the sky over Austerlitz dwarfs his dreams of glory?

NATASHA ROSTOVA

Natasha’s vitality, mistakes, love, humiliation, and recovery embody Tolstoy’s interest in spontaneous life. She changes dramatically without becoming reducible to one moral lesson.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is Natasha’s impulsiveness both a source of danger and a sign of life?

FAMILY LIFE

The Rostovs and Bolkonskys offer contrasting domestic worlds. Tolstoy repeatedly finds historical meaning in meals, dances, marriages, births, debts, and household decisions.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does ordinary family life carry as much weight as military history in the novel?

AUSTERLITZ

The battle exposes the confusion between plans and events. Commanders imagine control, but smoke, terrain, fear, timing, rumor, and countless individual actions produce outcomes no single mind directs.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does battle reveal the limits of centralized intention?

BORODINO

Borodino becomes Tolstoy’s great demonstration that historical events emerge from innumerable decisions and conditions rather than one commander’s genius.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Tolstoy resist assigning victory or defeat to a single decision?

NAPOLEON

Napoleon represents the illusion that power consists in personally causing vast events. Tolstoy’s irony repeatedly contrasts Napoleon’s self-image with the limited causal reach of any individual.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is Napoleon’s belief in his own historical agency philosophically important to the novel?

KUTUZOV

Kutuzov’s wisdom lies partly in recognizing the limits of command. He waits, listens, and avoids pretending to control what cannot be controlled.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Is restraint a form of leadership, and how does Tolstoy distinguish it from passivity?

CAPTIVITY AND PLATON KARATAEV

Pierre’s captivity strips away social abstractions. Karataev’s simple presence becomes a model of acceptance, connectedness, and immediate life.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Tolstoy locate spiritual insight in a character with little formal philosophy?

HISTORY AND FREE WILL

The essays argue that historical causation resembles infinitesimal calculus: countless small actions combine into large movements. Freedom is experienced personally even within networks of necessity.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How can Tolstoy preserve meaningful individual life while denying that individuals control history?

THE EPILOGUE

The novel ends in domestic life and philosophical reflection rather than heroic closure. History continues through families, institutions, desires, and unseen causal chains.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is domestic continuity a fitting conclusion to an epic about war and history?

REVIEW — TEN CLAIMS TO REMEMBER

1. State the work’s central problem in one sentence.
2. Identify its strongest argument, insight, or dramatic achievement.
3. Distinguish description from endorsement.
4. Track the work’s major causal, conceptual, or character relationships.
5. Identify one historical assumption the work depends on.
6. Explain one major term, image, experiment, or conflict in the work’s own framework.
7. Connect the work to at least two earlier Great Books.
8. State the strongest objection or criticism.
9. Identify one insight that survives even if some conclusions fail.
10. Explain why the work still belongs in a Great Books curriculum.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Which claim from this work most changes the way you understand its subject?

DEEPER READING LABS

LAB 1 — ARGUMENT OR PLOT MAP
Map the work’s central movement. For argument, identify the thesis and strongest supports. For literature, identify the decisive choices, reversals, and consequences.

LAB 2 — KEY DISTINCTIONS
Identify five distinctions or oppositions the work relies on and explain what becomes difficult to understand if each is blurred.

LAB 3 — HISTORICAL TEST
Choose one historical assumption, institution, scientific limitation, or social practice and evaluate how much the work depends on it.

LAB 4 — STRONGEST OBJECTION
Construct the strongest objection you can, then answer it using evidence from the work itself.

LAB 5 — GREAT BOOKS CONNECTION
Compare this work with at least two earlier authors on freedom, morality, nature, history, society, science, religion, or human character.
