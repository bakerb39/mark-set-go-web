ABOUT HABAKKUK

Habakkuk is a dialogue between a troubled prophet and God. The prophet’s central problem is moral: Why does God tolerate injustice? Then, when God announces Babylon as the instrument of judgment, Habakkuk asks an even harder question: How can God use a nation more wicked than Judah?

The book moves from complaint to watchful faith and ends with one of Scripture’s strongest declarations of trust without visible prosperity.

HOW TO READ THIS BOOK

Read the biblical text before the guide section whenever possible. Pay attention to repeated words, images, accusations, promises, changes in speaker, and how judgment and hope relate. The prophets address real historical situations, but their theological message often reaches beyond the immediate crisis.

1. HABAKKUK 1 — “HOW LONG?”

Habakkuk sees violence, injustice, and failed law.

He asks:
How long will I cry for help?

This is faithful protest.

Habakkuk assumes God is just, which is precisely why injustice troubles him.

Biblical faith can question circumstances because it trusts God’s character.

STUDY QUESTION
Why can asking “How long?” be an expression of faith rather than unbelief?

2. GOD’S SURPRISING ANSWER — BABYLON

God says he is raising the Chaldeans/Babylonians.

Habakkuk is stunned.

His first problem:
Why does God tolerate evil?

His second problem:
Why would God use worse evil to judge it?

This is one of Scripture’s clearest examples of providence being morally complex.

God can use a human power without approving that power’s arrogance.

STUDY QUESTION
Why does God’s use of Babylon not make Babylon righteous?

3. HABAKKUK 2 — THE RIGHTEOUS SHALL LIVE BY FAITH

Habakkuk waits.

God tells him to write the vision.

Then comes the famous line:
“The righteous shall live by his faith/faithfulness.”

Paul later uses this text in Romans and Galatians.

Hebrews also uses it to encourage endurance.

The immediate idea is steadfast trust while waiting for God’s justice.

Faith does not require immediate visible resolution.

STUDY QUESTION
What does it mean to live by faith while the world still looks unjust?

4. THE FIVE WOES

Habakkuk condemns:
greed,
violent empire,
unjust gain,
shaming others,
and idolatry.

Babylon will not escape judgment.

The instrument of judgment becomes the object of judgment because moral accountability remains universal.

STUDY QUESTION
Why is no political power exempt simply because God providentially uses it?

5. HABAKKUK 3 — “YET I WILL REJOICE”

The final prayer remembers God’s mighty acts.

Then Habakkuk imagines total economic collapse:
no figs,
no grapes,
no olives,
no crops,
no sheep,
no cattle.

“Yet I will rejoice in the LORD.”

This is not prosperity-based faith.

Habakkuk’s joy rests in God himself.

That is mature worship.

STUDY QUESTION
What would remain of your faith if visible prosperity disappeared?

MAJOR THEMES

THEODICY — Faith wrestles honestly with evil.
PROVIDENCE — God’s use of nations does not erase their guilt.
FAITH — The righteous endure by trust.
JUSTICE — Oppressive power is temporary.
WORSHIP — God remains worthy even when circumstances collapse.

HOW THIS BOOK POINTS FORWARD TO CHRIST

Paul quotes Habakkuk 2:4 in Romans 1 and Galatians 3 when explaining righteousness by faith.

Hebrews 10 uses the same passage to call suffering Christians to endurance.

Habakkuk’s waiting faith becomes part of the New Testament’s language for trusting God’s saving work in Christ.

DIFFICULT PASSAGES AND READING CAUTIONS

FAITH VS. FAITHFULNESS — The Hebrew word can carry both trust and steadfast faithfulness. New Testament use develops the text christologically and soteriologically.

“YET I WILL REJOICE” — Do not use this passage to shame grieving believers. Habakkuk reaches joy after honest complaint, not by skipping lament.

BABYLON — God’s sovereignty over Babylon does not make atrocities good.

WHOLE-BOOK REVIEW

1. What is Habakkuk’s first complaint?
2. Why does God’s answer create a second complaint?
3. What does Habakkuk 2:4 say?
4. What sins does Babylon commit?
5. How does the book end?
6. Why is Habakkuk important to Paul?

FINAL SYNTHESIS:
Explain how Habakkuk moves from “How long?” to “Yet I will rejoice.”
