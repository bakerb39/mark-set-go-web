JOSHUA
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide
Biblical book: Joshua

Land, promise, conquest, covenant faithfulness, leadership, inheritance, judgment, and the difficult theology of Canaan.

HOW TO USE THIS BIBLE GUIDE
Use this guide alongside the biblical text. Read the assigned chapters first whenever possible. The guide is designed to help with structure, historical and literary context, theology, difficult passages, connections across Scripture, discussion, and comprehension—not to replace Scripture itself.

READING PRINCIPLES
Pay attention to what the text actually says, distinguish description from approval, read each passage in its literary and covenant context, and allow clearer passages and the wider biblical canon to inform difficult ones.

PURPOSE AND PLACE IN SCRIPTURE

Joshua narrates Israel’s entrance into Canaan under Joshua, the defeat of major enemies, distribution of tribal inheritances, and covenant renewal. It presents fulfillment of promises while confronting readers with difficult accounts of warfare and judgment.

STUDY QUESTION
How does the book hold together gift, obedience, judgment, and inheritance?

THE CANAANITE QUESTION

The conquest is one of the Bible’s most morally difficult subjects. The text frames it as divine judgment within a unique covenant-historical setting, not as a general authorization for religious violence.

STUDY QUESTION
What safeguards are necessary to prevent the conquest narratives from being universalized into human policy?
