SONG OF SOLOMON
Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide

ABOUT THIS BOOK

Song of Solomon is biblical love poetry celebrating desire, beauty, longing, mutual delight, sexuality, and the power of love. The woman’s voice is especially prominent.

Jewish and Christian traditions have also read the Song symbolically in relation to God and his people, but the poem’s celebration of human love should be understood before allegorical application.

BOOK STRUCTURE

Song 1–2 — attraction and delight
Song 3–5 — longing, union, searching
Song 6–8 — renewed praise, exclusivity, love’s power

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Read the biblical text first whenever possible. Then use the guide to trace the argument, narrative movement, theology, historical context, difficult passages, and connections to the rest of Scripture. The goal is serious understanding without turning the guide into a technical commentary.

HISTORICAL, LITERARY, AND READING CONTEXT

HISTORICAL AND LITERARY CONTEXT

The Song belongs to biblical wisdom/poetic literature and has generated unusually diverse interpretation.

Premodern Jewish and Christian interpreters often emphasized allegory, seeing God and Israel or Christ and the church. Modern literary interpretation has recovered the importance of the poem’s human erotic dimension.

These need not be enemies, but order matters: understand the human love poetry before building theological analogy.

A POSITIVE THEOLOGY OF DESIRE

Much Christian teaching about sexuality focuses on prohibition. The Song contributes something equally necessary: delight.

Bodies are praised.
Desire is voiced.
Love is mutual.
Beauty is noticed.
Longing is powerful.
Exclusivity matters.

The Song therefore belongs in Christian sexual ethics because holiness is not merely saying no to sin; it also means learning to say yes to love in its proper covenantal form.

READING THIS BOOK WITH THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

A strong reading of Song Of Solomon moves in three directions.

FIRST: READ BACKWARD.

Ask what earlier Scripture this book assumes. Covenant promises, creation, Exodus, law, priesthood, kingship, wisdom, and earlier family history often give later passages their meaning.

SECOND: READ FORWARD.

Ask what problems remain unresolved. Does the book achieve final rest? Does the king remain faithful? Does sacrifice permanently remove sin? Does wisdom conquer death? Does reform change the heart? The unresolved questions become part of the Bible’s forward movement.

THIRD: READ CANONICALLY.

Follow how later biblical writers actually use the book. This is more reliable than inventing symbolic connections. When the New Testament quotes, echoes, or develops a passage, pay attention to both continuity and transformation.

This three-direction method helps Christian readers avoid two mistakes: treating the Old Testament as if Christ were irrelevant, or treating every Old Testament detail as a secret code whose only purpose was to predict Jesus.

HOW THIS BOOK POINTS FORWARD TO CHRIST

The New Testament does not directly interpret the Song christologically.

Christian tradition has nevertheless used covenant marriage as an analogy for Christ and the church.

That analogy can be meaningful when it grows out of the Bible’s broader marriage imagery rather than denying the Song’s first-level celebration of human love.

DIFFICULT PASSAGES AND READING CAUTIONS

SEXUAL IMAGERY — The Song is intentionally sensual. Avoid either prudish denial or crude over-literalization.

MARRIAGE STATUS — Interpreters debate precise narrative setting and speaker identities.

SOLOMON — References do not settle whether Solomon is the primary lover or a literary frame.

ALLEGORY — Valuable as reception history, but should not erase literary meaning.
