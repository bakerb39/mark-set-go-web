ESTHER
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide
Biblical book: Esther

Diaspora, hidden providence, courage, identity, reversal, political danger, deliverance, and the origin of Purim.

HOW TO USE THIS BIBLE GUIDE
Use this guide alongside the biblical text. Read the assigned chapters first whenever possible. The guide is designed to help with structure, historical and literary context, theology, difficult passages, connections across Scripture, discussion, and comprehension—not to replace Scripture itself.

READING PRINCIPLES
Pay attention to what the text actually says, distinguish description from approval, read each passage in its literary and covenant context, and allow clearer passages and the wider biblical canon to inform difficult ones.

PURPOSE AND PLACE IN SCRIPTURE

Esther tells how the Jewish people in the Persian Empire escape a genocidal plot through a chain of reversals involving Esther and Mordecai. God is never explicitly named in the Hebrew text, making providence, human courage, and hiddenness central interpretive themes.

STUDY QUESTION
Why might a biblical book about deliverance deliberately avoid explicitly naming God?

CHRIST AND THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

Esther preserves the covenant people through whom the larger biblical promises continue. Christian readers may see patterns of providence and deliverance without turning Esther into a direct allegory of Christ.

STUDY QUESTION
