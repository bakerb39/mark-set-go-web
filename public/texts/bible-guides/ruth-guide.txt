RUTH
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide
Biblical book: Ruth

Loyalty, providence, redemption, family, foreignness, generosity, Davidic ancestry, and quiet faithfulness.

HOW TO USE THIS BIBLE GUIDE
Use this guide alongside the biblical text. Read the assigned chapters first whenever possible. The guide is designed to help with structure, historical and literary context, theology, difficult passages, connections across Scripture, discussion, and comprehension—not to replace Scripture itself.

READING PRINCIPLES
Pay attention to what the text actually says, distinguish description from approval, read each passage in its literary and covenant context, and allow clearer passages and the wider biblical canon to inform difficult ones.

PURPOSE AND PLACE IN SCRIPTURE

Ruth is a compact story of famine, bereavement, migration, loyalty, provision, marriage, and family restoration during the chaotic era of the judges. Its ordinary acts of faithfulness become part of the ancestry of David.

STUDY QUESTION
Why is Ruth’s quiet domestic story placed against the violent background of Judges?

CHRIST AND THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

Matthew includes Ruth in Jesus’ genealogy. Christian interpretation also sees themes of redemption, covenant loyalty, and inclusion converging in Christ without reducing Boaz to a simplistic one-to-one allegory.

STUDY QUESTION
How does Ruth expand the reader’s understanding of who can be incorporated into God’s covenant purposes?

MAJOR THEMES

Hesed—steadfast covenant loyalty—providence, generosity, redemption, inclusion, family, land, and faithful ordinary action dominate the book.

STUDY QUESTION
