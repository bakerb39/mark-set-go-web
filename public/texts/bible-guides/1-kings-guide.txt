1 KINGS
Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide

ABOUT THIS BOOK

1 Kings begins with Solomon’s glory and temple, then traces the monarchy’s division and decline. The book asks whether wisdom, wealth, political power, and religious institutions can preserve a people whose hearts turn from God.

BOOK STRUCTURE

1 Kings 1–11 — Solomon
1 Kings 12–16 — divided kingdom
1 Kings 17–19 — Elijah
1 Kings 20–22 — Ahab, Naboth, final conflict

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Read the biblical text first whenever possible. Then use the guide to trace the argument, narrative movement, theology, historical context, difficult passages, and connections to the rest of Scripture. The goal is serious understanding without turning the guide into a technical commentary.

HISTORICAL, LITERARY, AND READING CONTEXT

HISTORICAL AND LITERARY CONTEXT

1 Kings begins near the height of Israel’s political power and ends with divided kingdoms, entrenched idolatry, and a prophetic struggle against royal corruption.

Solomon’s story is intentionally paradoxical. He possesses extraordinary wisdom yet violates the very principles that wisdom should protect. Wealth, horses, wives, and international prestige accumulate in ways Deuteronomy had warned kings about.

The divided kingdom is therefore not a random political accident; it is connected to long spiritual and leadership failures.

KINGS AND PROPHETS

As royal power becomes less trustworthy, prophetic confrontation becomes increasingly important.

Elijah can stand before Ahab precisely because the king is not morally ultimate.

This is one of the Bible’s great contributions to political theology: rulers govern, but the word of God judges rulers.

READING THIS BOOK WITH THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

A strong reading of 1 Kings moves in three directions.

FIRST: READ BACKWARD.

Ask what earlier Scripture this book assumes. Covenant promises, creation, Exodus, law, priesthood, kingship, wisdom, and earlier family history often give later passages their meaning.

SECOND: READ FORWARD.

Ask what problems remain unresolved. Does the book achieve final rest? Does the king remain faithful? Does sacrifice permanently remove sin? Does wisdom conquer death? Does reform change the heart? The unresolved questions become part of the Bible’s forward movement.

THIRD: READ CANONICALLY.

Follow how later biblical writers actually use the book. This is more reliable than inventing symbolic connections. When the New Testament quotes, echoes, or develops a passage, pay attention to both continuity and transformation.

This three-direction method helps Christian readers avoid two mistakes: treating the Old Testament as if Christ were irrelevant, or treating every Old Testament detail as a secret code whose only purpose was to predict Jesus.

HOW THIS BOOK POINTS FORWARD TO CHRIST

Jesus presents himself as greater than Solomon.

He invokes Elijah’s ministry and identifies prophetic rejection patterns.

The failed monarchy and compromised temple create longing for a wiser king and truer dwelling of God.
