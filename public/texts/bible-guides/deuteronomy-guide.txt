DEUTERONOMY
Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide

ABOUT THIS BOOK

Deuteronomy is Moses’ covenant sermon to Israel on the edge of the promised land. A new generation must remember what God has done, love him wholeheartedly, obey his commands, reject idolatry, practice justice, and choose life.

The book is not a second random law collection. It is covenant renewal shaped around memory, love, loyalty, and future faithfulness.

BOOK STRUCTURE

Deuteronomy 1–4 — remembering the wilderness
Deuteronomy 5–11 — covenant, Ten Commandments, Shema
Deuteronomy 12–26 — covenant laws for life in the land
Deuteronomy 27–30 — blessing, curse, exile, return, choice
Deuteronomy 31–34 — succession, song, blessing, death of Moses

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Read the biblical text first whenever possible. Then use the guide to trace the argument, narrative movement, theology, historical context, difficult passages, and connections to the rest of Scripture. The goal is serious understanding without turning the guide into a technical commentary.

HISTORICAL, LITERARY, AND READING CONTEXT

HISTORICAL AND LITERARY CONTEXT

Deuteronomy is structured as Moses addressing Israel in Moab before entry into Canaan. The old wilderness generation has largely died. A new generation must hear the covenant as its own.

The book often resembles ancient covenant or treaty forms: historical review, stipulations, blessings and curses, witnesses, and succession. But the relationship is not cold diplomacy. Moses repeatedly speaks of love, heart, memory, and loyalty.

Deuteronomy becomes enormously influential in the rest of the Bible. Kings are evaluated by it, prophets echo it, exile is interpreted through it, and Jesus quotes it repeatedly.

READING DEUTERONOMY CHRISTIANLY

Notice how often Moses moves between “remember” and “do not forget.” Forgetfulness is not an intellectual lapse; it is living as though grace were self-achievement.

Also distinguish Israel’s national covenant from universal moral principles. Some commands regulate a land-based ancient society. Others directly express enduring truths about worship, justice, love, and loyalty. The New Testament helps Christians discern how those categories are fulfilled and transformed in Christ.

READING THIS BOOK WITH THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

A strong reading of Deuteronomy moves in three directions.

FIRST: READ BACKWARD.

Ask what earlier Scripture this book assumes. Covenant promises, creation, Exodus, law, priesthood, kingship, wisdom, and earlier family history often give later passages their meaning.

SECOND: READ FORWARD.

Ask what problems remain unresolved. Does the book achieve final rest? Does the king remain faithful? Does sacrifice permanently remove sin? Does wisdom conquer death? Does reform change the heart? The unresolved questions become part of the Bible’s forward movement.

THIRD: READ CANONICALLY.

Follow how later biblical writers actually use the book. This is more reliable than inventing symbolic connections. When the New Testament quotes, echoes, or develops a passage, pay attention to both continuity and transformation.

This three-direction method helps Christian readers avoid two mistakes: treating the Old Testament as if Christ were irrelevant, or treating every Old Testament detail as a secret code whose only purpose was to predict Jesus.

HOW THIS BOOK POINTS FORWARD TO CHRIST

Jesus quotes Deuteronomy repeatedly during his temptation.

He names the Shema as the greatest commandment.

Acts identifies Jesus as the prophet like Moses.

Paul uses Deuteronomy in discussions of faith, law, and righteousness.

The promised circumcision of the heart anticipates new-covenant transformation.

Jesus is therefore presented as faithful Israel, greater Moses, and authoritative teacher.

DIFFICULT PASSAGES AND READING CAUTIONS

WAR AND CONQUEST — Deuteronomy contains commands tied to Israel’s unique covenant and land setting. They are not standing mandates for religious warfare.

BLESSING AND PROSPERITY — Covenant blessings in Israel’s land should not be converted into promises that obedient Christians will always be wealthy.

LAW APPLICATION — Christians read Deuteronomy through Christ and the new covenant rather than applying ancient national law directly.

DIVORCE LAW — Deuteronomy 24 regulates a broken social reality; Jesus later distinguishes regulation from God’s creational ideal.
