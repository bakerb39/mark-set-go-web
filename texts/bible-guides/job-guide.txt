JOB
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide
Biblical book: Job

Suffering, justice, wisdom, lament, divine freedom, human limitation, friendship, and faithful speech before God.

HOW TO USE THIS BIBLE GUIDE
Use this guide alongside the biblical text. Read the assigned passages first whenever possible. The guide is designed to help with structure, literary context, theology, difficult passages, connections across Scripture, discussion, and comprehension—not to replace Scripture itself.

READING PRINCIPLES
Pay attention to genre. Poetry, wisdom sayings, lament, dialogue, and reflective argument communicate differently from narrative or legal prose. Distinguish description from endorsement, read passages in context, and allow the whole biblical canon to inform difficult texts.

PURPOSE AND PLACE IN SCRIPTURE

Job explores the problem of righteous suffering through narrative, poetry, debate, lament, wisdom, and divine speech. The book refuses the simple equation that suffering always proves personal guilt.

STUDY QUESTION
Why is Job’s innocence essential to the book’s challenge to conventional wisdom?

Job is introduced as righteous. In the heavenly council, the Accuser challenges the integrity of Job’s faith, and Job loses property, children, and health.

JOB 3 — JOB’S LAMENT

Job curses the day of his birth but does not curse God. His lament opens a space for honest grief within faith.

THE FRIENDS’ BASIC ARGUMENT

Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar repeatedly defend a retributive moral order: God is just, therefore Job’s suffering must correspond to hidden sin.

STUDY QUESTION

JOB’S RESPONSE

Job insists on his integrity, longs to present his case before God, and oscillates among confidence, despair, protest, and hope.

JOB 38–41 — GOD ANSWERS

CHRIST AND THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

Job contributes to the Bible’s broader theology of innocent suffering, lament, intercession, wisdom, and trust. Christian readers also place Job alongside the ultimate innocent sufferer, Christ, without turning every detail into direct prediction.

STUDY QUESTION
How does Job prevent Christians from treating suffering as reliable evidence of God’s displeasure?

DIFFICULT PASSAGES TO STUDY

The heavenly council, the death of Job’s children, Job’s harsh words, the friends’ theology, Elihu, God’s speeches, Behemoth and Leviathan, and the ending all require careful attention.

STUDY QUESTION
Does the ending erase Job’s losses, or does it restore life without undoing grief?

WHOLE-BOOK REVIEW

1. Summarize the book’s place in the biblical storyline or wisdom tradition.
2. Identify its major literary divisions or recurring forms.
3. Explain how genre affects interpretation.
4. State the book’s central teaching about God.
5. Explain what the book reveals about human experience, suffering, wisdom, worship, work, love, or mortality.
6. Identify three key passages and explain why they matter.
7. Explain at least two important connections to later Scripture.
8. Identify one difficult passage and describe the context needed to interpret it responsibly.
9. State one major Great Idea this book contributes to syntopical study.
10. Summarize the book in one paragraph without reducing it to a slogan.
