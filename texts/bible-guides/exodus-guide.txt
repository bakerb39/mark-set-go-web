EXODUS
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide
Biblical book: Exodus

Deliverance, Passover, covenant, law, divine presence, worship, and Israel’s formation as God’s people.

HOW TO USE THIS BIBLE GUIDE
Use this guide alongside the biblical text. Read the assigned chapters first whenever possible. The guide is designed to help with structure, historical and literary context, theology, difficult passages, connections across Scripture, discussion, and comprehension—not to replace Scripture itself.

READING PRINCIPLES
Pay attention to what the text actually says, distinguish description from approval, read each passage in its literary and covenant context, and allow clearer passages and the wider biblical canon to inform difficult ones.

PURPOSE AND PLACE IN SCRIPTURE

Exodus moves from slavery in Egypt to covenant at Sinai and the construction of the tabernacle. God reveals his name, redeems Israel, gives his law, and comes to dwell among his people.

STUDY QUESTION
Why does Exodus connect liberation with covenant obligation rather than independence alone?

EXODUS 1–2 — SLAVERY AND MOSES

Pharaoh oppresses Israel and orders the death of Hebrew sons. Moses is preserved, raised in Pharaoh’s household, flees after killing an Egyptian, and begins life in Midian.

STUDY QUESTION

EXODUS 3–4 — THE BURNING BUSH

God calls Moses and reveals the divine name associated with 'I AM.' Moses repeatedly objects, while God promises presence and equips him for the mission.

EXODUS 5–11 — PHARAOH AND THE PLAGUES

The conflict with Pharaoh becomes a contest over worship, lordship, and the knowledge of God. The plagues expose Egypt’s power as unable to resist the God of Israel.

EXODUS 12 — PASSOVER

Israel is spared through the Passover sacrifice and leaves Egypt. The event becomes a permanent act of remembrance in Israel’s worship.

CHRIST AND THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

The New Testament draws heavily on Exodus imagery: Passover, deliverance, baptism through the sea, manna, covenant, priesthood, tabernacle, and God dwelling with his people.

STUDY QUESTION
Why does the Exodus become one of Scripture’s central patterns for salvation?

DIFFICULT PASSAGES TO STUDY

The hardening of Pharaoh, the death of Egypt’s firstborn, slavery laws, divine violence, and detailed cultic regulations require careful historical and canonical reading.

STUDY QUESTION
How should divine judgment be read alongside Exodus’s equally strong revelation of mercy?

3. Name the most important people, events, laws, speeches, or institutions.
4. Explain the book’s central teaching about God.
5. Explain what the book reveals about human sin, faith, obedience, or community.
6. Identify the book’s major covenant themes.
7. Explain at least two important connections to later Scripture.
