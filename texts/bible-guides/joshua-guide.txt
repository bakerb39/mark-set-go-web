ABOUT THIS BOOK

Conquest, covenant faithfulness, land, courage, remembrance, and the transition from wilderness to settlement.

BOOK STRUCTURE

Joshua 1–5 — entry and preparation

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE

Read the biblical text first whenever possible. Then use the guide to trace the argument, narrative movement, theology, historical context, difficult passages, and connections to the rest of Scripture. The goal is serious understanding without turning the guide into a technical commentary.

HISTORICAL, LITERARY, AND READING CONTEXT

HISTORICAL AND LITERARY CONTEXT

Joshua continues directly from Deuteronomy. Moses is dead, Joshua leads, and Israel crosses into the land promised to Abraham.

The book combines battle narrative, theological summary, geographic allotment, covenant ceremony, and farewell speeches. The land lists are not an appendix; they are the concrete fulfillment of centuries-old promises.

At the same time, Joshua itself acknowledges incomplete possession. That tension prevents the book from being read as though every promise had reached its final form.

READING THE CONQUEST CAREFULLY

The conquest is the central ethical challenge. The book presents Israel as an instrument of divine judgment in a unique covenant setting. Christians must never turn those commands into authorization for later conquest, colonialism, ethnic cleansing, or religious war.

Also notice Rahab and the Gibeonites. The story itself complicates any reading based on ethnic superiority. Outsiders can respond in faith and be incorporated, while Israelites such as Achan can fall under judgment.

READING THIS BOOK WITH THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

A strong reading of Joshua moves in three directions.

FIRST: READ BACKWARD.

Ask what earlier Scripture this book assumes. Covenant promises, creation, Exodus, law, priesthood, kingship, wisdom, and earlier family history often give later passages their meaning.

SECOND: READ FORWARD.

Ask what problems remain unresolved. Does the book achieve final rest? Does the king remain faithful? Does sacrifice permanently remove sin? Does wisdom conquer death? Does reform change the heart? The unresolved questions become part of the Bible’s forward movement.

THIRD: READ CANONICALLY.

Follow how later biblical writers actually use the book. This is more reliable than inventing symbolic connections. When the New Testament quotes, echoes, or develops a passage, pay attention to both continuity and transformation.

This three-direction method helps Christian readers avoid two mistakes: treating the Old Testament as if Christ were irrelevant, or treating every Old Testament detail as a secret code whose only purpose was to predict Jesus.

HOW THIS BOOK POINTS FORWARD TO CHRIST

Jesus bears the Hebrew/Greek form of the same name as Joshua: “YHWH saves.”

Hebrews contrasts Joshua’s land-rest with a greater rest still available in Christ.

Rahab enters the messianic genealogy.

Joshua’s incomplete rest therefore points beyond itself.

DIFFICULT PASSAGES AND READING CAUTIONS

CONQUEST — This is the major ethical difficulty. Christians interpret the commands as unique acts of divine judgment tied to Israel’s covenant-land mission, not permission for later religious conquest. Ancient war rhetoric may also use conventional totalizing language.

RAHAB’S LIE — Scripture praises her faith and protection of the spies; Christians differ on evaluating the deception itself.

LAND PROMISE — Modern political application requires care and cannot simply leap from ancient covenant text to contemporary policy.
