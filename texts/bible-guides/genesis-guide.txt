GENESIS
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide
Biblical book: Genesis

Beginnings, creation, fall, covenant, promise, patriarchs, providence, and the origins of Israel.

HOW TO USE THIS BIBLE GUIDE
Use this guide alongside the biblical text. Read the assigned chapters first whenever possible. The guide is designed to help with structure, historical and literary context, theology, difficult passages, connections across Scripture, discussion, and comprehension—not to replace Scripture itself.

READING PRINCIPLES
Pay attention to what the text actually says, distinguish description from approval, read each passage in its literary and covenant context, and allow clearer passages and the wider biblical canon to inform difficult ones.

PURPOSE AND PLACE IN SCRIPTURE

Genesis opens the biblical story by explaining the creation of the world, the entrance of human rebellion, the spread of sin, and God’s covenant promises through Abraham’s family. Its two great movements are primeval history in Genesis 1–11 and patriarchal history in Genesis 12–50.

STUDY QUESTION
How do the creation narratives and the patriarchal narratives belong to one theological story?

GENESIS 1–2 — CREATION

God creates an ordered world and declares it good. Humanity is made in the image of God and given a vocation within creation. Genesis 2 narrows the focus to the human pair, the garden, work, relationship, and divine command.

STUDY QUESTION

GENESIS 3 — THE FALL

The serpent’s temptation questions God’s word and goodness. The human pair disobeys, shame and alienation enter human relationships, and the ground itself becomes associated with frustration and death.

STUDY QUESTION

GENESIS 4–11 — SIN SPREADS

Cain and Abel, the genealogies, the flood, and Babel show violence, pride, corruption, judgment, preservation, and renewed human ambition. The narrative repeatedly moves from human disorder to divine judgment and mercy.

GENESIS 6–9 — NOAH AND THE FLOOD

The flood presents both devastating judgment and preservation. God establishes a covenant with Noah and with living creatures, symbolized by the bow in the clouds.

GENESIS 11 — BABEL

Humanity seeks a name and security through centralized achievement. God scatters the peoples and confuses their language.

GENESIS 12–25 — ABRAHAM

God calls Abram, promises land, offspring, and blessing, and forms a covenant with him. Abraham’s life combines remarkable trust with serious failures, showing that the promise depends ultimately on God’s faithfulness.

GENESIS 15 AND 17 — COVENANT

Genesis 15 dramatizes God’s covenant commitment; Genesis 17 gives circumcision as the covenant sign and renames Abram and Sarai.

GENESIS 22 — THE BINDING OF ISAAC

Abraham is commanded to offer Isaac, the promised son, and God provides a substitute. The episode is one of Scripture’s most difficult and influential tests of faith.

GENESIS 25–36 — ISAAC AND JACOB

The promise continues through a deeply imperfect family marked by favoritism, deception, rivalry, and divine persistence. Jacob is eventually renamed Israel.

GENESIS 37–50 — JOSEPH

Joseph is betrayed, enslaved, falsely accused, elevated in Egypt, and ultimately positioned to preserve his family during famine. Human evil and divine providence converge without becoming morally identical.

MAJOR THEMES

Creation, image of God, sin, judgment, blessing, covenant, election, faith, promise, providence, land, offspring, and the blessing of the nations form the theological vocabulary that later Scripture develops.

CHRIST AND THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

The New Testament repeatedly returns to Adam, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph. Christian interpretation sees the promise of blessing to the nations reaching its fulfillment in Christ while also treating Genesis as foundational to creation, sin, faith, and covenant.

STUDY QUESTION

DIFFICULT PASSAGES TO STUDY

Creation chronology, the sons of God in Genesis 6, the flood’s scope, election, the destruction of Sodom, Abraham and Isaac, and the moral failures of the patriarchs deserve careful attention to genre, context, and the whole biblical canon.
