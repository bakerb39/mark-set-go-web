1 SAMUEL
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Bible Guide
Biblical book: 1 Samuel

Samuel, Saul, David, kingship, prophetic authority, obedience, covenant leadership, and the transition from judges to monarchy.

HOW TO USE THIS BIBLE GUIDE
Use this guide alongside the biblical text. Read the assigned chapters first whenever possible. The guide is designed to help with structure, historical and literary context, theology, difficult passages, connections across Scripture, discussion, and comprehension—not to replace Scripture itself.

READING PRINCIPLES
Pay attention to what the text actually says, distinguish description from approval, read each passage in its literary and covenant context, and allow clearer passages and the wider biblical canon to inform difficult ones.

PURPOSE AND PLACE IN SCRIPTURE

1 Samuel describes Israel’s transition from the era of the judges to monarchy. Samuel emerges as prophet and judge; Saul becomes Israel’s first king and fails; David is anointed and gradually rises while refusing to seize the throne unlawfully.

STUDY QUESTION
Why does the book evaluate kings primarily by their response to God rather than by military success?

Hannah’s prayer, Samuel’s birth, dedication, and prophetic call introduce themes of reversal, faithful prayer, corrupt leadership, and God raising the lowly.

1 SAMUEL 4–7 — THE ARK AND GOD’S HOLINESS

CHRIST AND THE REST OF SCRIPTURE

The book develops expectations for an anointed king under God’s authority. The term 'anointed' becomes foundational for later messianic hope fulfilled in Christian interpretation in Jesus the Messiah.

STUDY QUESTION
How does the contrast between Saul and David sharpen the biblical ideal of kingship?

DIFFICULT PASSAGES TO STUDY

The request for a king, the Amalek command, Saul’s troubling spirit, David’s violence, the medium at Endor, and divine judgment require attention to covenant and narrative context.

STUDY QUESTION
Which passage most clearly warns against confusing divine permission with divine approval?
