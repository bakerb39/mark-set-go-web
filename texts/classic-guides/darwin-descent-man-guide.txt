THE DESCENT OF MAN
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Original work by Charles Darwin

A guide to human evolution, sexual selection, morality, mind, race, and the scientific and ethical difficulties of Darwin’s second major evolutionary work.

ABOUT THIS GUIDE
This independently written educational guide is designed for serious first reading, rereading, and seminar study. It follows the Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide pattern with focused sections, discussion prompts, section quizzes, Great Ideas, review, and whole-guide quiz hooks.

AT A GLANCE

Darwin extends evolutionary reasoning explicitly to human beings. He argues for continuity between humans and other animals in anatomy, mind, emotion, and ancestry, while also developing sexual selection as a major evolutionary force.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why was applying common descent to humans more socially explosive than applying it to other species?

HUMAN ANCESTRY

Comparative anatomy, embryology, vestigial structures, and disease susceptibility connect humans to other animals. Darwin’s argument seeks natural continuity rather than a separate biological origin for humanity.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does continuity challenge ideas that treat humans as biologically outside nature?

MENTAL CONTINUITY

Darwin compares memory, curiosity, imitation, emotion, social behavior, and problem solving across species. He argues that many differences are of degree rather than absolute kind.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What follows philosophically if human mental capacities developed from earlier animal capacities?

THE MORAL SENSE

Darwin explains morality through social instincts, memory, reflection, habit, and approval. Sympathy can expand beyond immediate kin as social life becomes more complex.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Can an evolutionary account of moral capacities explain how morality arose without deciding which moral judgments are correct?

SEXUAL SELECTION

Traits may evolve because they increase mating success rather than survival. Competition within one sex and preferences in the other can generate ornaments, weapons, displays, and behavioral differences.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why can a costly trait spread if it improves reproductive success?

HUMAN SEXUAL SELECTION

Darwin applies sexual selection to human physical and cultural differences. Some claims reflect Victorian assumptions about gender and must be read critically rather than treated as settled science.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How can a scientific theory be powerful while particular applications remain distorted by social assumptions?

RACE AND HUMAN UNITY

Darwin rejects the idea that human races are separately created species and emphasizes common ancestry. At the same time, the book uses racial hierarchies and nineteenth-century language that modern science rejects.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How should readers distinguish Darwin’s common-descent argument from the racial assumptions embedded in parts of his presentation?

LANGUAGE AND CULTURE

Language, imitation, social learning, and culture amplify biological capacities. Human evolution therefore cannot be understood solely in terms of anatomy.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does culture become both a product of evolved capacities and a new force shaping human behavior?

EMOTION AND EXPRESSION

Darwin connects facial expressions and emotional responses across peoples and species, reinforcing his broader argument for continuity.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD

SCIENCE AND SOCIAL MISUSE

Later movements often labeled Social Darwinism or eugenics drew selective lessons from evolutionary theory. Biological description does not automatically justify political hierarchy or coercive policy.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is moving from 'is' to 'ought' especially dangerous when evolutionary claims concern human groups?

MODERN EVALUATION

Genetics and genomics strongly support common human ancestry and reveal far more variation within populations than simplistic racial typologies imply. Modern evolutionary science retains common descent and sexual selection while rejecting many Victorian classifications.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What does scientific self-correction look like when a foundational work contains both enduring theory and obsolete claims?

LEGACY

The Descent of Man forced biology, philosophy, theology, and social thought to confront humanity as part of evolutionary history. Its importance includes both its breakthroughs and the need to read its historical prejudices critically.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How should a Great Books curriculum handle influential works whose scientific achievements coexist with morally troubling assumptions?

REVIEW — TEN CLAIMS TO REMEMBER

1. State the work’s central problem in one sentence.
2. Identify its strongest argument, insight, or dramatic achievement.
3. Distinguish description from endorsement.
4. Track the work’s major causal, conceptual, or character relationships.
5. Identify one historical assumption the work depends on.
6. Explain one major term, image, experiment, or conflict in the work’s own framework.
7. Connect the work to at least two earlier Great Books.
8. State the strongest objection or criticism.
9. Identify one insight that survives even if some conclusions fail.
10. Explain why the work still belongs in a Great Books curriculum.

DEEPER READING LABS

LAB 1 — ARGUMENT OR PLOT MAP
Map the work’s central movement. For argument, identify the thesis and strongest supports. For literature, identify the decisive choices, reversals, and consequences.

LAB 2 — KEY DISTINCTIONS
Identify five distinctions or oppositions the work relies on and explain what becomes difficult to understand if each is blurred.

LAB 3 — HISTORICAL TEST
Choose one historical assumption, institution, scientific limitation, or social practice and evaluate how much the work depends on it.

LAB 4 — STRONGEST OBJECTION
Construct the strongest objection you can, then answer it using evidence from the work itself.

LAB 5 — GREAT BOOKS CONNECTION
Compare this work with at least two earlier authors on freedom, morality, nature, history, society, science, religion, or human character.
