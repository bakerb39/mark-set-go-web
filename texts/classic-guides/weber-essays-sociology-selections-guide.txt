AT A GLANCE

Weber analyzes modern society through concepts such as rationalization, bureaucracy, legitimacy, class, status, vocation, and the relationship between ideas and institutions. He resists single-cause explanations and emphasizes historically specific combinations of meaning and structure.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Weber avoid reducing social change to economics alone?

SOCIAL ACTION

Sociology seeks to interpret meaningful action: people act not merely because forces push them but because situations carry meanings for them.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why must explanation of human action include interpretation of meaning?

IDEAL TYPES

Concepts such as bureaucracy or charismatic authority are ideal types—analytical constructions that emphasize features useful for comparison rather than perfect descriptions of reality.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is an ideal type useful precisely because no real case matches it completely?

RATIONALIZATION

Modern societies increasingly organize activity through calculation, rules, predictability, technical expertise, and formal procedures.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What forms of freedom are gained and lost through rationalization?

BUREAUCRACY

Bureaucracy is technically powerful because it relies on specialized offices, records, rules, hierarchy, and trained officials. Its efficiency can also create rigidity and depersonalization.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Weber regard bureaucracy as both indispensable and threatening?

TRADITIONAL AUTHORITY

Traditional authority rests on the sanctity of inherited customs and established status relationships.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What makes obedience to tradition different from obedience to law?

CHARISMATIC AUTHORITY

Charisma rests on devotion to an exceptional person believed to possess extraordinary qualities. It can disrupt established institutions but must eventually be routinized to endure.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is charisma politically unstable once the leader disappears?

LEGAL-RATIONAL AUTHORITY

Modern authority increasingly rests on impersonal rules and offices rather than personal loyalty. People obey the legally constituted office, not merely the individual holder.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does impersonal authority strengthen legitimacy while reducing personal discretion?

CLASS, STATUS, PARTY

Economic class, social prestige, and political organization are distinct dimensions of power. Weber therefore complicates purely class-based models of stratification.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why might people with similar incomes occupy very different status positions?

POLITICS AS A VOCATION

Politics requires passion, responsibility, and judgment. Weber distinguishes an ethic of ultimate ends from an ethic of responsibility attentive to consequences.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does responsible political action require more than moral purity of intention?

SCIENCE AS A VOCATION

Modern scholarship demands specialization and intellectual discipline. Science can clarify facts and consequences but cannot by itself determine ultimate values.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Weber deny that science can settle every question worth asking?

LEGACY

Weber became foundational to sociology, political science, organizational theory, and studies of modernity. His enduring concern is the fate of meaning and freedom inside increasingly rationalized institutions.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Where do you most clearly see Weber’s 'iron cage' problem in contemporary life?

REVIEW — TEN CLAIMS TO REMEMBER

1. State the work’s central problem in one sentence.
2. Identify its strongest argument, insight, narrative achievement, or explanatory move.
3. Distinguish description from endorsement.
4. Track the work’s major causal, conceptual, historical, or character relationships.
5. Identify one historical assumption or limitation.
6. Explain one major term, image, experiment, or distinction in the work’s own framework.
7. Connect the work to at least two earlier Great Books.
8. State the strongest objection or criticism.
9. Identify one insight that survives even if some conclusions fail.
10. Explain why the work still belongs in a Great Books curriculum.
