A DOLL’S HOUSE
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Original work by Henrik Ibsen

A guide to marriage, identity, money, gender, law, performance, and the dramatic force of Nora’s final decision.

ABOUT THIS GUIDE
This independently written educational guide is designed for serious first reading, rereading, and seminar study. It follows the Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide pattern with focused sections, discussion prompts, section quizzes, Great Ideas, review, and whole-guide quiz hooks.

HOW TO USE IT
Read one section at a time. Use Discuss with reading companion after each section for interpretation and Quiz me for a short comprehension check. Save the full-guide quiz for the end.

AT A GLANCE

A Doll’s House turns an apparently comfortable marriage into an examination of dependence, secrecy, law, gender expectations, and self-education. Nora’s concealed loan becomes the mechanism through which the structure of the Helmer marriage is exposed.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does a financial secret reveal the deeper architecture of Nora and Torvald’s relationship?

THE DOLL HOUSE

The title suggests a beautiful but controlled domestic space. Nora has been treated first as her father’s doll-child and then as Torvald’s doll-wife.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What does the doll metaphor reveal about affection that lacks equality?

NORA

Nora initially performs the cheerful, irresponsible wife expected of her, yet the loan shows independence, courage, and practical competence hidden beneath the performance.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is Nora both more capable and less self-aware than she first appears?

TORVALD

Torvald’s language is affectionate but patronizing. He values reputation, order, and his identity as protector. His response to the crisis shows that his ideal of marriage depends on hierarchy.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What does Torvald fear losing when the secret is exposed?

THE LOAN

Nora illegally signs her father’s name to obtain money that saves Torvald’s life. The act combines love, deception, legal vulnerability, and economic exclusion.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How should moral judgment change when the law restricts the options available to a person?

KROGSTAD

Krogstad is both threat and mirror. He has also committed forgery and seeks restoration of social respectability. His character complicates any easy division between villain and victim.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Ibsen give the apparent antagonist motives that parallel Nora’s?

MRS. LINDE

Kristine’s economic experience and directness contrast with Nora’s protected domestic life. Her relationship with Krogstad offers a different model of partnership based on truth and shared work.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does Mrs. Linde expose the artificiality of Nora’s marriage simply by living differently?

DR. RANK

Rank’s illness, unspoken love, and family history introduce mortality and inherited consequences beneath the comedy of manners.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does the play place disease and death inside an apparently festive domestic setting?

THE TARANTELLA

Nora’s frantic dance is performance, distraction, panic, and a visible sign of inner crisis. Torvald directs her even as she tries to delay his discovery.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How does the tarantella compress the entire marriage into a theatrical image?

THE LETTERBOX

Control of information becomes literal: Torvald owns the key, while Nora desperately tries to delay access. Domestic authority and narrative suspense merge.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is control over information a form of power in the play?

THE FINAL CONVERSATION

After Torvald’s first reaction reveals his priorities, Nora reevaluates the entire marriage. She concludes that she must educate herself before she can be an adequate wife or mother.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does Nora’s decision emerge from a change in self-understanding rather than from a new romantic attachment?

THE DOOR SLAM

The closing exit became one of modern drama’s defining moments. It does not answer what Nora’s future will be; its force lies in making uncertainty the condition of self-determination.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is an unresolved departure more powerful than a neatly completed new life?

REVIEW — TEN CLAIMS TO REMEMBER

1. State the work’s central problem in one sentence.
2. Identify its strongest argument, insight, or dramatic achievement.
3. Distinguish description from endorsement.
4. Track the work’s major causal, conceptual, or character relationships.
5. Identify one historical assumption the work depends on.
6. Explain one major term, image, experiment, or conflict in the work’s own framework.
7. Connect the work to at least two earlier Great Books.
8. State the strongest objection or criticism.
9. Identify one insight that survives even if some conclusions fail.
10. Explain why the work still belongs in a Great Books curriculum.

DEEPER READING LABS

LAB 1 — ARGUMENT OR PLOT MAP
Map the work’s central movement. For argument, identify the thesis and strongest supports. For literature, identify the decisive choices, reversals, and consequences.

LAB 2 — KEY DISTINCTIONS
Identify five distinctions or oppositions the work relies on and explain what becomes difficult to understand if each is blurred.

LAB 3 — HISTORICAL TEST
Choose one historical assumption, institution, scientific limitation, or social practice and evaluate how much the work depends on it.

LAB 4 — STRONGEST OBJECTION
Construct the strongest objection you can, then answer it using evidence from the work itself.

LAB 5 — GREAT BOOKS CONNECTION
Compare this work with at least two earlier authors on freedom, morality, nature, history, society, science, religion, or human character.
