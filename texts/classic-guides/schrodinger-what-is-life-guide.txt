WHAT IS LIFE?
An Independent Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide
Original work by Erwin Schrödinger

A guide to heredity, order, entropy, the gene, the aperiodic crystal, and the influence of physics on molecular biology.

This independently written educational guide is designed for serious first reading, rereading, and seminar study. It follows the Mark, Set, Go! Classic Guide pattern with focused sections, discussion prompts, section quizzes, Great Ideas, review, and whole-guide quiz hooks.

AT A GLANCE

Schrödinger asks how living organisms maintain ordered structure and transmit hereditary information with remarkable stability despite thermal disorder at molecular scales. The book helped inspire later molecular biologists even though some details were speculative or incomplete.

THE APERIODIC CRYSTAL

He proposes that the gene may resemble an aperiodic crystal: a stable molecular structure whose nonrepeating arrangement can encode complex information.

SMALL NUMBERS AND STABILITY

At molecular scales, random thermal fluctuations are significant. Schrödinger asks how hereditary mechanisms remain reliable despite operating with relatively small numbers of atoms.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why is biological reliability surprising when microscopic events are noisy?

ORDER FROM ORDER

Organisms reproduce highly organized structures from existing organized information. This differs from familiar statistical order emerging from enormous numbers of random molecular events.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD

NEGATIVE ENTROPY

Schrödinger describes organisms as maintaining organization by feeding on 'negative entropy.' Modern thermodynamics would express this more carefully through exchanges of energy and entropy with the environment.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
Why does local biological order not violate the second law of thermodynamics?

MUTATION

Changes in hereditary material must be stable enough to persist yet rare enough to preserve continuity. Schrödinger links mutation to discrete molecular changes.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD

QUANTUM THEORY AND BIOLOGY

The book speculates that quantum principles may help explain the stability of genetic structures. Its main influence was not a complete quantum theory of life but a new style of physical thinking about biology.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD

INFLUENCE ON MOLECULAR BIOLOGY

Scientists including James Watson and Francis Crick later cited the book as intellectually stimulating. The actual discovery of DNA structure depended on genetics, chemistry, crystallography, and experimental biology far beyond Schrödinger’s discussion.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How should we distinguish inspiration from direct scientific causation?

WHAT THE BOOK GOT WRONG

Genes are not simple crystals, biological information is molecularly richer than the book could know, and some thermodynamic language is outdated. Yet the core intuition that heredity depends on stable molecular information proved powerful.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
How can an imperfect scientific book still help create a research program?

LIFE AND PHYSICS

Schrödinger asks whether life requires new physical laws or can ultimately be understood through known physics applied to extraordinarily organized systems.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD

LEGACY

What Is Life? helped bridge physics and biology at a moment when molecular genetics was emerging. Its enduring lesson is methodological: fundamental questions can become visible when one discipline looks carefully at another.

QUESTION TO CARRY FORWARD
What current scientific field might benefit from a similarly productive cross-disciplinary question?
